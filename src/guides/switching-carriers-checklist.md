Switching carriers takes about 20 minutes once you have the right information. Most problems come from skipping one of the steps below.

## Before you switch

1. **Check your device balance.** If you are on a payment plan or bill credits, leaving can make the remaining balance due. See [bill credits decoded]({{ROOT}}guides/free-phone-bill-credits-explained/).
2. **Confirm your phone is unlocked** or will be unlocked by your current carrier. See [unlocked vs carrier phones]({{ROOT}}guides/unlocked-vs-carrier-phones/).
3. **Check compatibility** with the new carrier using their device checker.
4. **Test coverage** where you live and work; an eSIM trial line is ideal.
5. **Note your billing cycle date.** Many carriers do not prorate the last month.

## Information you need to port your number

- Your account number with the current carrier.
- A **number transfer PIN** (most carriers generate this in their app or website).
- The name and billing address on the account.

**Do not cancel your old service first.** Porting your number to the new carrier cancels the old line automatically. Cancelling first can release your number.

## Switching day

1. Start the new service and choose "keep my number" or "port in."
2. Enter the account number and transfer PIN.
3. Install the new eSIM or SIM. The port usually completes within minutes to a few hours.
4. Test calls, texts, data and voicemail. Set up voicemail again on the new carrier.
5. Update two-factor authentication apps if any rely on the carrier.

## After you switch

- Watch for a final bill from the old carrier and confirm any credits.
- Return any leased equipment, such as hotspots, if required.
- Remove old carrier apps and AutoPay.

## FAQ

### How long does porting take?
Often minutes for mobile-to-mobile ports, occasionally up to a business day.

### Will I lose my texts and contacts?
No. They live on your phone and cloud backup, not on the carrier.

### Can I switch in the middle of a billing cycle?
Yes, but you may pay for the full month with the old carrier. Time the switch near your cycle date when possible.
