International roaming used to be the single most painful line on a phone bill. Today you have three good options, and the right choice depends on trip length, data needs and whether you need your home number.

## Option 1: Your carrier's roaming pass

Many carriers sell daily international passes or include roaming on premium plans. It is the easiest option: nothing to install, and your number works normally. The catch is cost on longer trips, because daily charges add up quickly.

## Option 2: A local SIM

Buying a SIM at your destination is often the cheapest per gigabyte, especially for long stays. Downsides: finding a shop, ID requirements in some countries, and losing access to your home number unless your phone supports dual SIM.

## Option 3: A travel eSIM

Travel eSIM providers sell data packages by country, region or globally, installed by scanning a QR code or through an app. You can buy before you leave and land connected.

- **Pros:** instant, no shop visit, often far cheaper than roaming, keeps your physical SIM or main eSIM active for calls and texts.
- **Cons:** usually data-only (no local number), validity windows, and some "unlimited" packages slow down after a daily threshold.

## Setup checklist

1. **Confirm your phone supports eSIM** and is unlocked. See [unlocked vs carrier phones]({{ROOT}}guides/unlocked-vs-carrier-phones/).
2. **Install the eSIM at home** on Wi-Fi, but do not activate data roaming on it until you land.
3. **Label lines** ("Home" and "Travel") and set Travel as the cellular data line.
4. **Keep Home on for calls and texts** if you need verification codes, but turn its data roaming off to avoid charges.
5. **Download offline maps** and a translation pack as backup.

## How much data do you need?

Maps, messaging and light browsing use roughly 0.3–0.7GB per day. Streaming and video calls push well past 1GB per day. Hotel and café Wi-Fi can cover heavy use.

## FAQ

### Will I still receive texts from home?
Yes, if your home line stays active alongside the travel eSIM. Your carrier may charge for incoming texts abroad, so check.

### Can I use a travel eSIM for calls?
Most travel eSIMs are data-only. Use internet calling apps over the data connection.

### What if my phone is locked?
A carrier-locked phone may reject other eSIMs. Ask your carrier about unlocking before you travel.
