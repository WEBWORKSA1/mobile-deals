An MVNO (mobile virtual network operator) is a carrier that does not own towers. It buys capacity wholesale from a network owner and resells it under its own brand. Mint Mobile, Tello and Google Fi run on T-Mobile's network; Visible runs on Verizon's. Because MVNOs skip retail stores and big marketing budgets, they can charge far less.

## What you keep

- **The same coverage map** as the host network in most areas.
- **5G access** on most modern MVNO plans.
- **Your number** — porting in is standard.
- **Your phone** if it is unlocked and compatible.

## What can differ

### Priority during congestion
Networks rank traffic. When a tower is busy (stadiums, rush hour, dense downtowns), lower-priority traffic slows first. Many MVNO lines sit below the host carrier's premium postpaid customers. In everyday use you may never notice; in crowded places you might.

### Roaming
Some MVNOs do not include domestic roaming on partner networks in rural areas where the host carrier has gaps. Check coverage where you actually travel.

### Support
Most MVNOs are online-first: chat and app support, few or no stores. If you like walking into a store, factor that in.

### Extras
Hotspot caps, international roaming and smartwatch support vary widely. Compare features in the [Plan Finder]({{ROOT}}plans/).

## Who should switch to an MVNO

- Anyone using under 30GB a month who is happy with their current network's coverage.
- People who buy phones outright or keep them for years.
- Second lines, kids' lines and backup lines.

## Who might stay with a big carrier

- Heavy users in very congested areas who need consistent top speeds.
- People who value in-store support.
- Families who benefit from postpaid device promotions they would use anyway.

## FAQ

### Will my phone work on an MVNO?
If it is unlocked and supports the host network's bands, almost certainly. Most carriers offer a compatibility checker.

### Is MVNO coverage worse?
The map is the same as the host network in most places; differences appear mainly in congestion and rural roaming.

### Can I switch back later?
Yes. Most MVNOs are month-to-month, so you can port your number out any time.
