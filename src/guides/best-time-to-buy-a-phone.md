Phone pricing is predictable. Manufacturers launch on a yearly rhythm, and retailers discount the outgoing model to clear stock. If you can wait a few weeks, you can often buy the same phone for much less.

## The yearly rhythm

| Period | What usually happens | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| January–February | Samsung's Galaxy S launch window; previous S models discounted | Buy last year's Galaxy S, or preorder bonuses on new |
| March–April | Mid-range launches (Galaxy A, Pixel a-series in some years) | Good time for budget phones |
| July | Large online summer sales events; foldable launches | Accessories and previous-gen Android deals |
| August | Google Pixel launch window; back-to-school promos | Previous Pixel discounts |
| September | Apple iPhone launch | Buy the outgoing iPhone, or carrier trade-in promos |
| October | Festival sales in India; early holiday deals | India buyers: watch festival price cuts |
| Late November | Black Friday and Cyber Monday | Broadest discounts across brands and carriers |
| December 26 | Boxing Day (Canada, UK, Australia) | Strong unlocked and plan promos |

Exact dates move every year, so treat this as a guide rather than a guarantee.

## Three timing rules

1. **Never buy a phone the month before its successor launches** unless the discount is deep.
2. **The best value is often the previous flagship** in the first three months after a new launch.
3. **Plan deals peak at year-end and during switching season** when carriers chase new lines.

## FAQ

### Is Black Friday really the cheapest time?
It is usually the broadest sale, but launch-season discounts on outgoing models can be just as deep.

### Should I preorder a new phone?
Only if the preorder bonus has real value to you. Prices almost always fall later.

### How fast do phones lose value?
Flagships commonly lose a large share of their value in the first year, then depreciate more slowly.
