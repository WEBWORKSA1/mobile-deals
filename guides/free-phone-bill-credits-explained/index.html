"Free" Phone Deals Decoded: How Bill Credits Really Work

A free phone is usually a 24–36 month loan paid back with monthly credits. Here is the math, the traps and when it is genuinely a good deal.

Updated November 19, 2026 · 7 min read · By the Mobile.Deals team

Carriers advertise flagship phones "on us" almost every week. The phone is real, but the free part is spread out. Understanding the mechanics takes five minutes and can save you from a very expensive surprise.

How a bill-credit deal works

  1. You buy the phone on a device payment plan, usually at full retail price.
  2. The carrier applies a monthly credit to your bill that cancels the device payment, typically over 24 or 36 months.
  3. To keep receiving credits, you must keep the line active on a qualifying plan, often a pricier unlimited tier, frequently with AutoPay and sometimes a number port-in or trade-in.

If you leave early, the credits stop and the remaining device balance becomes due. On a $1,000 phone with 36 monthly credits, leaving after 12 months can mean roughly $667 owed.

The real cost formula

Compare two paths over the same horizon:

  • Path A ("free" phone): qualifying plan price × months + any upfront fees, taxes and activation charges.
  • Path B (buy unlocked): phone price + cheaper plan price × months.

Example over 36 months: a qualifying plan at $85/month costs $3,060. Buying a $799 phone outright and using a $25/month plan costs $799 + $900 = $1,699. The "free" phone costs $1,361 more.

The deal flips in your favour only when you would have chosen that premium plan anyway. Run your own numbers in the True Cost Calculator.

Fine print worth reading

  • Trade-in requirements: many offers need a specific trade-in model and condition.
  • New line vs upgrade: the best credits are often reserved for new lines or switchers.
  • Plan downgrades: moving to a cheaper plan mid-term can cancel or reduce credits.
  • Locked device: the phone usually stays locked to the carrier until paid off.
  • Prepaid card rebates: some offers pay you via a virtual prepaid card instead of credits, with a claim deadline.

When bill-credit deals win

  • You already want that carrier's premium plan for features such as international data or streaming bundles.
  • You keep phones for 3+ years anyway, so the lock-in costs you nothing.
  • The offer stacks a trade-in credit that beats what a buyback site would pay. Check with our Trade-in Estimator.

When to walk away

  • The qualifying plan costs $30+ more per month than what you need.
  • You might switch carriers within two years.
  • You are adding a line you do not need just to qualify.

FAQ

Do I pay taxes on a free phone?

Often yes. Many US carriers charge sales tax on the full retail price at purchase, even when credits cover the phone.

Can I pay off the phone early to unlock it?

Usually you can pay the remaining balance, but you lose the remaining credits, so do the math first.

Are bill credits the same as a discount?

No. A discount lowers the price once; credits are conditional and stop if you leave or change plans.
