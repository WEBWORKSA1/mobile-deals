How to Choose a Phone Plan: A 5-Step Framework That Saves Real Money

Stop guessing. Measure your data, pick the network that covers you, then compare plans on total cost, not the headline price.

Updated November 19, 2026 · 7 min read · By the Mobile.Deals team

Most people pick a phone plan the way they pick a restaurant on a road trip: whatever sign is biggest. That is how a single person ends up paying $85 a month for an unlimited plan while using 9GB. This framework takes about ten minutes and usually finds $10–$40 a month per line.

Step 1: Measure what you actually use

Open your phone's settings and look at cellular data for the last full billing cycle (iPhone: Settings → Cellular; Android: Settings → Network → Data usage). Check three months if you can, because one heavy travel month can mislead you.

  • Under 5GB: you live on Wi-Fi. A small prepaid plan is almost always the winner.
  • 5–20GB: the sweet spot for mid-tier plans. This is where the biggest savings hide.
  • Over 25GB, or you hotspot a laptop: unlimited starts to make sense.

No history? Use our Data Usage Calculator to estimate from your daily habits.

Step 2: Pick the network before the plan

A cheap plan on a network that drops calls at your home is not cheap. In the US, nearly every plan runs on one of three networks: AT&T, T-Mobile or Verizon. Budget brands (called MVNOs) rent capacity on those same towers. Ask neighbours and coworkers which network works in your building, your commute and your workplace, then shortlist plans on that network. Our MVNO guide explains the trade-offs.

Step 3: Decide what extras you will really use

Hotspot allowance, international calling, streaming bundles and smartwatch lines are where premium plans justify their price. Be honest: if you have not used a streaming bundle in six months, do not pay $20 a month for it. List your must-haves and ignore everything else.

Step 4: Compare total cost, not the sticker

Headline prices hide conditions. Watch for:

  • Prepay pricing: a $15 plan may require paying 12 months upfront.
  • Introductory rates: prices that rise after three months.
  • AutoPay discounts: often $5–$10 per line, sometimes only with a debit card.
  • Taxes and fees: postpaid plans often add several dollars per line; many prepaid plans include them.
  • Device lock-in: a "free" phone tied to 24–36 months of bill credits. See bill credits decoded.

Multiply the real monthly cost by 24 and compare that number. Our Plan Finder does this automatically and sorts by 24-month total.

Step 5: Test before you commit

Many prepaid carriers offer short trial periods or let you buy a single month. Use an eSIM as a second line on your current phone for a week, check coverage where you live and work, then port your number once you are happy.

A quick worked example

A single user averaging 11GB a month is paying $75 on a big-carrier unlimited plan. A 15GB prepaid plan at $20 a month on the same underlying network costs $480 over 24 months, versus $1,800 for the current plan. That is $1,320 saved with zero change in coverage.

FAQ

Is unlimited data worth it?

Only if you regularly use more than about 25GB a month or rely on hotspot for work. Most people use far less than they think.

Will a cheaper plan have worse coverage?

Not necessarily. Many budget plans use the same towers as the big three; the difference is usually priority during congestion.

How often should I review my plan?

Once a year, or whenever your usage changes. Prices in this market move every few months.
